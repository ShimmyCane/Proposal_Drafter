# S+Y Studio Proposal Framework

## What the past proposals consistently include

After reviewing the sample proposals, the recurring structure is:

1. Introduction and project context
2. Project description and objectives
3. Scope of work broken into phases
4. Deliverables and design outputs
5. Fee and payment schedule
6. Timeline and milestone expectations
7. Assumptions, exclusions, and clarifications
8. General conditions and closing terms

This pattern works well for architecture, tenant improvement, residential, and permitting-focused proposals.

## How this framework should reflect S+Y Studio

This framework should reflect the way S+Y Studio works:

- Collaborative and transparent: the proposal should feel like a clear partnership from the start.
- Modeling-led and design-forward: the studio should make space for modeling, visual clarity, and early design communication.
- Clear up front: expectations, scope, and decision points should be explained early to reduce confusion.
- Fixed-fee oriented: proposals should generally be structured around a fixed fee, while still grounding the estimate in a realistic project schedule and fee logic.
- Revision-conscious: two rounds of revisions are a reasonable and professional default.
- Flexible and client-friendly: rather than scaring clients with too many exclusions, the studio can maintain a broad library of additional services that can be pulled in as needed.
- Professional and persuasive: the tone should feel polished, confident, and client-centered.

## Recommended proposal structure

### 1. Cover Letter / Introduction
Include:
- Date
- Client name and project address
- Brief statement of intent
- Project type and overall goal
- Studio role and point of contact

Template:
- "We are pleased to submit our proposal for [project name] at [address]."

### 2. Project Description
Include:
- Project summary
- Site context and existing conditions
- Client goals and design priorities
- Key constraints such as schedule, budget, permitting, or phasing
- Any known consultant coordination requirements

Template:
- Project type: [new build / renovation / TI / ADU / mixed-use / permitting support]
- Existing conditions: [describe current site/building]
- Goal: [describe desired outcome]
- Constraints: [schedule, budget, code, approvals, occupancy target]

### 3. Scope of Work
Organize by phase. Common phases include:
- Pre-design / site analysis
- Schematic design
- Design development
- Construction documentation
- Permit submission / plan check support
- Construction administration

For each phase, include:
- What services are provided
- Estimated duration
- Deliverables
- Expected client coordination

Suggested structure:
- Phase I: [name] – [fee], [estimated duration]
- Phase II: [name] – [fee], [estimated duration]
- Phase III: [name] – [fee], [estimated duration]

### 4. Deliverables
List the expected outputs clearly, for example:
- Site plan
- Floor plans
- Elevations
- Sections
- Reflected ceiling plans
- Details and schedules
- Permit-ready drawings
- Renderings or mood boards (if included)
- Consultant coordination documents

### 5. Fee and Payment Schedule
Include:
- Total fee for the proposed scope
- Deposit or retainer requirement
- Milestone-based payments
- Hourly rates for additional services
- Reimbursable costs if applicable
- A note that the fee is grounded in a realistic schedule and anticipated project effort

Common payment language:
- Deposit to begin work
- Payment upon submission of drawings
- Payment upon RTI / permit submission / completion of phase

Suggested pricing approach:
- Use fixed-fee pricing as the default structure
- Cross-check the fee against the project schedule and anticipated hours
- Keep the pricing logic transparent enough that the client understands the basis of the fee
- Use a flexible additional-services library rather than overloading the base scope with exclusions

### 6. Timeline / Schedule
Include:
- Estimated duration for each phase
- Major milestones
- Potential permitting or review delays
- Schedule assumptions and dependencies

Useful language:
- Estimated [X–Y weeks/months]
- Timeline is dependent on client feedback and jurisdiction review
- Expedited review may reduce delays

### 7. Assumptions, Clarifications, and Exclusions
This section is critical. It prevents scope confusion.

Include:
- What is included
- What is excluded
- Client responsibilities
- Consultant responsibilities
- Permit fees and jurisdictional costs
- Number of design revisions included
- Additional services billed hourly
- Timing assumptions for client decisions and consultant input

Typical exclusions:
- Structural engineering
- MEP engineering and stamping
- Civil / landscape / irrigation design
- Survey / geotechnical report
- Renderings beyond basic concept studies
- Construction administration unless explicitly included
- Permit fees unless stated otherwise

Suggested approach:
- Keep the base scope clear and welcoming
- Use a library of optional additional services for items that may arise later
- Define revision limits clearly, with two rounds of revisions as the default
- Avoid making the proposal feel overly restrictive by listing too many exclusions upfront

### 8. General Conditions
Include:
- Collaboration method (for example, shared Box folder)
- Ownership of documents and instruments of service
- Reuse restrictions for documents
- Termination terms
- Payment terms and late fees
- Proposal validity period

### 9. Signature Block
Include:
- Client name
- Architect / studio name
- Date lines
- Optional contact details

## S+Y Studio-specific drafting notes

For S+Y Studio proposals, it helps to emphasize:
- Design intent and project narrative
- Clear distinction between base scope and additional services
- Realistic schedule assumptions tied to permitting and coordination
- Transparent scope boundaries for consultants and owner responsibilities
- Milestone-based fee structure to reduce ambiguity
- A strong note on the client’s decision-making role during design phases
- A collaborative, confident, and professional tone that feels welcoming rather than overly legalistic
- A clear explanation that modeling and visual communication are part of the studio’s design approach

---

## Minimal working checklist

Use this checklist before sending a proposal:
- [ ] Project description is clear
- [ ] Scope is broken into phases
- [ ] Deliverables are explicit
- [ ] Fee and payment schedule are stated
- [ ] Timeline is realistic
- [ ] Included vs excluded services are clear
- [ ] Consultant and owner responsibilities are defined
- [ ] General conditions are included
- [ ] Proposal reflects the studio’s collaborative and design-forward approach
- [ ] Proposal tone feels professional, persuasive, and welcoming
- [ ] Proposal is reviewed for consistency with the scope
